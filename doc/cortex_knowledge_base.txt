CORTEX: An Advanced Corpus Query System Integrating NLP and AI for Linguistic Analysis
Abstract
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) represents major methodological advancements in corpus linguistics frontier. While traditional software has long provided robust mechanisms for token frequency and concordancing, linguists today increasingly require tools capable of performing additional tasks such as automated data collection from the internet and annotation beyond POS tagging. This paper introduces CORTEX, an advanced corpus query system offered as an alternative to othee corpus query systems. We detail how CORTEX serves as an all-encompassing linguistic workstation by providing an automatic YouTube Corpus Downloader, sentiment annotation, natural language querying interfaces, and customisable collocation pattern grammars. Furthermore, CORTEX introduces the capability to generate automatic dictionary drafts, extract simultaneous multi-file keyword lists, run inferential statistical testing directly on corpus variables, and provide generative AI interpretation for all analytical outputs. By seamlessly blending traditional empirical rigor with modern machine learning, CORTEX is a new alternative to fulfill the need of today corpus linguists.
1. Introduction
The landscape of corpus linguistics has been shaped by the corpus query systems. These systems used to be very expensive, could only be accessed by scholars working in well-funded institutions and big publishers. For these reasons, access to corpus data and tools was highly restricted, and corpus-based methods were not widely available to the broader research community. Scholars then created corpus query systems that can be installed on users' personal computers or accessed only using the internet. This has had a big impact on the development of corpus linguistics. In terms of analytic features, recent years have seen major developments from core functionalities such as concordance, collocation, and keywords to various intelligent tools for corpus data and metadata indexing and visualisation. 
In the fields of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Artificial Intelligence (AI), scholars have developed sophisticated tools to assist corpus linguists. A web scraper, for example, can be used to collect data from social media such as Reddit or Twitter very quickly. A corpus linguist, even with minimum technical knowledge, can use AI tools like ChatGPT or Gemini to incorporate metadata information into their corpora in XML format, so that the metadata can also be indexed by a corpus query system. However, in some cases, the workflow is disjoint. Users have to move from one tool to another.  CORTEX addresses this divide by functioning as an advanced, all-in-one corpus query system. Designed to integrate NLP and AI directly into the analytical interface, CORTEX empowers linguists to collect dynamic digital data, query text intuitively using natural language, and leverage AI for real-time interpretation—all without leaving the platform. This article explores the specific features and architectural innovations that position CORTEX at the forefront of modern linguistic analysis.
2. Streamlined Data Collection and Intelligent Preprocessing
2.1 The YouTube Corpus Downloader
Modern linguistic research increasingly relies on digital, user-generated discourse as a primary data source. Platforms like YouTube offer vast repositories of spoken language (via transcripts) and spontaneous written interaction (via comment sections). Recognizing the difficulty researchers face in harvesting this data, CORTEX features a built-in YouTube Corpus Downloader. This module allows researchers to instantly download video transcripts and user comments, converting them directly into raw or XML-annotated corpus texts ready for immediate ingestion. Users have granular control over data collection, allowing them to restrict the number of comments downloaded or opt to capture the entire discourse thread. This dramatically accelerates the data collection phase for sociolinguists and discourse analysts.
2.2 Transparent Corpus Architecture and XML Parsing
Upon data ingestion, CORTEX provides immediate structural transparency. The preprocessing engine reads both raw text and complex XML-marked-up files. Crucially, before any analysis begins, CORTEX explicitly displays the corpus architecture to the user. Every XML attribute value present in the markup is automatically indexed and converted into a definable sub-corpus. This transparency allows users to understand the precise boundaries and variables of their data immediately, utilizing these XML attributes to execute highly restricted, intersectional searches.
2.3 Interactive AI-Driven Sentiment Annotation
A major innovation in CORTEX's preprocessing pipeline is its integration of sentence-level semantic tagging. During ingestion, text is automatically segmented into sentences, and each sentence is assigned a sentiment polarity (Positive, Negative, or Neutral) via an integrated NLP model. What distinguishes CORTEX from fully automated black-box systems is its human-in-the-loop design. Users are presented with these AI-generated sentiment tags and possess the ability to review and edit them to meet specific research expectations. If the user disagrees with an assigned sentiment, they can modify it; the system will not append the tags to the final database without user approval. Once accepted, these sentiment polarities become fully searchable sub-corpora. A researcher can, for instance, instantly restrict their concordance search to uncover the most frequent adjectives occurring exclusively within 'negative' sentiment sentences.
3. Intuitive and Multi-Layered Querying
3.1 Natural Language Query Translation
A significant barrier to entry in advanced corpus linguistics is the steep learning curve associated with Corpus Query Language (CQL). CORTEX eliminates this barrier through an AI-assisted Natural Language Query interface. A user can simply type a plain English request—such as "word 'the' followed by a noun"—and the AI will automatically translate this intent into the correct formal syntax (e.g., 'the _NN'). Because tagging conventions vary, the preprocessing module requires the user to specify their tagset mapping first (ensuring the system knows that 'NN' equals a noun in the current corpus). Users can request AI assistance to draft the query, manually refine the translated syntax, and execute complex structural searches without requiring formal programming knowledge.
3.2 Advanced Positional N-gram Analysis
While standard tools offer basic N-gram generation, CORTEX provides unparalleled flexibility by allowing users to display and filter N-grams independently based on Token, POS, or Lemma per positional item. If a researcher wishes to discover a specific three-word syntactic sequence (e.g., the word 'the' followed by any adjective and any noun), CORTEX allows them to set the display output to 'Token' but apply positional filters to 'POS'. The result displays the actual token realizations (e.g., 'the quick brown', 'the sudden drop') while strictly adhering to the 'the + JJ + NN' grammatical sequence. In other software, filtering and displaying disjointed linguistic layers simultaneously is either impossible or restrictively complex.
4. Deep Phraseology and Lexicography
4.1 Customizable Collocation Patterns (Grammars)
The analysis of phraseological tendencies often requires grouping collocates by grammatical function. While tools like Sketch Engine offer 'Word Sketches' to categorize collocates, these grammatical relations are hard-coded by the developers. CORTEX democratizes this process by allowing users to freely write and label their own 'Collocation Patterns' or grammar rules. For example, a user analyzing the phrase 'was studied by many scholars' (where 'studied' is the node and 'scholars' is the collocate) can write a custom rule such as: `BE NODE by (optional word) COLLOCATE`. The user can label this custom rule 'PASSIVE'. CORTEX will automatically categorize all matching collocates in the corpus under this 'PASSIVE' heading. This grants researchers total methodological independence to define grammatical relations without relying on third-party software updates.
4.2 Automatic Dictionary Drafting and Word Profiling
CORTEX functions not merely as an analytical tool, but as a complete lexicographical workstation. It features an Automatic Dictionary Drafting tool that transforms corpus data—frequencies, typical collocations, and exemplar concordance lines—into structured dictionary entries automatically. Unlike other systems that require exporting data to separate lexicography software, entire drafting workflows can be completed natively within CORTEX.
Additionally, the system features a robust Word Profiler. Researchers can apply existing standardized vocabulary frameworks or upload their own custom wordlists to assess the lexical complexity and vocabulary coverage of their texts, making it an invaluable asset for applied linguists and materials developers.
5. Advanced Statistics and AI Interpretation
5.1 Simultaneous Granular Keyword Extraction
Keyword analysis is a staple of corpus linguistics, used to determine the 'aboutness' of a text. Traditional tools often force researchers to extract keywords for the entire corpus collectively or process sub-corpora one by one. CORTEX streamlines this by allowing users to extract keywords for all texts or specific sub-corpora simultaneously. If a user uploads 20 distinct texts, they can instruct the system to restrict by file, generating and displaying 20 separate keyword lists on a single screen at once, vastly accelerating comparative analysis.
5.2 Built-In Inferential Statistical Testing
Moving beyond descriptive statistics, CORTEX features a dedicated Statistical Testing module that enables true inferential analysis. Leveraging frequency information drawn from Tokens, POS tags, Lemmas, or structural metadata, users can conduct significance testing or calculate Pearson correlations directly within the interface. Depending on the specific research question and the available metadata, this allows linguists to statistically validate their findings—such as determining if the frequency of a specific modal verb correlates significantly with the publication year of a text—without exporting data to statistical software like SPSS.
5.3 Automated Summarisation and AI Interpretation
To assist researchers in navigating massive datasets, CORTEX includes a Summarisation module. Users can select the entire corpus or highly specific sub-corpora and instantly generate macro-level text summaries, providing immediate contextual overviews of large document sets.
Finally, the crowning feature of CORTEX is its pervasive AI integration. Recognizing that statistical tables and complex collocations can be dense and difficult to parse, CORTEX provides an 'AI Interpretation' function for all analytical results. Whether viewing a concordance line, a Pearson correlation matrix, or a custom collocation pattern, users can prompt the built-in AI to generate a linguistically sound, plain-English interpretation of the data on the screen. This acts as an embedded research assistant, helping linguists hypothesize findings and articulate patterns.
6. Conclusion
CORTEX redefines the standard for what a corpus query system should be. By bridging the historical gap between traditional data science and applied linguistics, it provides a comprehensive environment where rigorous statistical testing meets the intuitive power of artificial intelligence. From the initial collection of YouTube data and transparent preprocessing to the execution of AI-translated natural language queries and customizable collocation grammars, CORTEX offers unprecedented methodological flexibility.
Most importantly, by embedding automated dictionary drafting, inferential statistics, and AI-driven interpretations directly into the workflow, CORTEX eliminates the need for fragmented, multi-software analyses. It empowers linguists to focus entirely on their research questions, supported by a tool that is as intelligent as it is robust.

CORTEX: Expanding Methodological Horizons in Corpus Linguistics through Integrated AI
Abstract
For decades, corpus linguistics has relied on a relatively stable set of methodological paradigms: frequency profiling, key word in context (KWIC) concordancing, and collocation extraction. While these methods excel at uncovering lexicogrammatical patterning, they often struggle to capture the broader semantic and pragmatic dimensions of discourse without intensive manual annotation. This paper introduces CORTEX, a hybrid corpus analysis platform that synthesizes traditional corpus linguistics with modern Natural Language Processing (NLP). Rather than focusing purely on computational architecture, this paper explores the linguistic affordances of CORTEX. We detail how the platform enables researchers to transition seamlessly from micro-level phraseological analysis to macro-level discourse mapping by natively incorporating sentence-level sentiment analysis (to trace semantic prosody), transformer-based topic modeling (for thematic clustering), and dynamic, multi-layered structural metadata filtering. By resolving the methodological divide between frequency-driven traditionalism and meaning-driven computational linguistics, CORTEX offers a robust toolkit for applied linguists, discourse analysts, and lexicographers to investigate language in use at unprecedented scales.
1. Introduction: The Methodological Evolution of Corpus Linguistics
Corpus linguistics is fundamentally defined by its empirical approach to language, relying on the quantitative observation of textual data to uncover qualitative linguistic insights. Since the late twentieth century, the software tools used to interrogate these corpora have profoundly shaped the types of research questions linguists can ask. The advent of foundational programs like WordSmith Tools and AntConc established the core methodological triad of the discipline: the wordlist, the concordance line, and the collocation table. These tools allowed researchers to move beyond intuition, demonstrating that language is heavily patterned and phraseologically driven.
As the field progressed, the compilation of mega-corpora containing billions of words necessitated a shift toward web-based platforms such as Sketch Engine and English-Corpora.org. These platforms introduced the capacity to execute complex Corpus Query Language (CQL) searches, allowing researchers to explore sophisticated colligational frameworks and lexico-syntactic dependencies. However, despite these advancements in scale, the underlying analytical paradigm remained largely tethered to the 'word-as-token' model. Traditional corpus tools excel at counting tokens and mapping syntactic proximity, but they remain largely opaque to the semantic, evaluative, and pragmatic dimensions of discourse. Investigating phenomena such as semantic prosody, thematic framing, or authorial stance traditionally requires researchers to manually code hundreds of concordance lines—a process that is labor-intensive and prone to subjective bias.
Furthermore, a profound methodological divide has emerged. While applied linguists continue to rely on standard concordancers, the field of computational linguistics has rapidly advanced into the realm of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP). NLP models can map dense semantic relationships, automatically summarize texts, and classify emotional valence. Yet, for the average corpus linguist, integrating these advanced semantic models requires exporting corpus data into programming environments like Python or R—a technically demanding workflow that fractures the analytical process.
Enter CORTEX: a corpus query system developed specifically to bridge this methodological divide. CORTEX is designed not merely as a faster concordancer, but as a holistic linguistic workstation. It embeds modern NLP capabilities directly into the traditional corpus interface, allowing linguists to explore lexicogrammar and semantics simultaneously. This paper outlines the linguistic capabilities of CORTEX, demonstrating how its integration of structural parsing, advanced dispersion metrics, semantic topic modeling, and sentiment analysis expands the boundaries of what is empirically observable in corpus linguistics.
2. Enhancing Lexicogrammatical and Phraseological Analysis
At its core, CORTEX respects the foundational methodologies of corpus linguistics, but it significantly deepens the analytical depth available to the researcher. By automatically verticalizing raw text through integrated NLP pipelines (such as Stanza), CORTEX ensures that every token is automatically assigned a lemma and a part-of-speech (POS) tag, immediately opening the door to generalized phraseological research without requiring the user to pre-annotate their data.
2.1 Intersectional Concordancing and Colligational Frameworks
The Concordance module in CORTEX transcends simple string matching. In linguistic research, isolating a specific sociolinguistic or diachronic variable is crucial. Because CORTEX parses and indexes deep hierarchical XML metadata during ingestion (e.g., text register, speaker demographic, publication year), researchers can perform highly targeted intersectional queries. A user can query a specific lemma, apply union-based filters to capture multiple morphosyntactic variants, and simultaneously restrict the sub-corpus dynamically using a graphical interface. This allows for rapid comparative concordancing—for instance, instantly comparing the usage of modal verbs between spoken and written registers without needing to partition the corpus files manually.
2.2 Rigorous Collocation and Lexical Association
The study of phraseology relies heavily on identifying statistically significant co-occurrences. The Collocation module within CORTEX computes a suite of association measures, including Log-Likelihood, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI), and T-scores. By offering multiple metrics, CORTEX allows lexicographers and phraseologists to distinguish between different types of collocations—for example, using PMI to identify highly specific, rare idiomatic pairings, or using T-scores to identify highly frequent, grammaticalized colligations. Furthermore, wildcard support within collocation spans allows linguists to uncover broader, discontinuous phraseological frames and semantic sequences.
2.3 Moving Beyond Raw Frequency: Dispersion and Keyness
A persistent issue in corpus linguistics is the over-reliance on raw frequency counts. A word may appear highly frequent in a corpus simply because it is repeated excessively in a single text, misleading researchers about its general utility. This is a critical problem for applied linguists involved in syllabus design and vocabulary acquisition.
To rectify this, the CORTEX Distribution module heavily emphasizes dispersion metrics, most notably Juilland’s D. Juilland’s D provides a coefficient of dispersion that indicates how evenly a lexical item is distributed across the texts comprising a corpus. By integrating Juilland's D with Zipf-band leveling and relative frequencies (Per Million Words), CORTEX allows applied linguists to construct pedagogically sound, highly generalizable vocabulary profiles that accurately reflect the core lexicon of a specific register or domain.
3. Bridging AI and Semantics: New Avenues for Discourse Analysis
The most significant methodological contribution of CORTEX is its ability to operationalize semantic and pragmatic phenomena at scale. By embedding AI algorithms directly into the corpus architecture, CORTEX automates analyses that would otherwise require exhaustive manual coding.
3.1 Semantic Prosody and Sentence-Level Sentiment Analysis
Semantic prosody—the tendency of a neutral word to collocate with items that express a positive or negative evaluation—is a notoriously difficult phenomenon to measure objectively. Traditionally, researchers must manually read hundreds of concordance lines to determine the evaluative environment of a node word.
CORTEX addresses this by embedding VADER (Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner) directly into the analytical pipeline. During ingestion, CORTEX utilizes custom NLP sentence-splitting logic to isolate complete semantic units, handling complex punctuation and trailing quotation marks with precision. It then assigns a sentiment polarity (Positive, Negative, Neutral) to every sentence in the corpus. For the corpus linguist, this means that semantic prosody can be quantified instantly. A researcher can query a node word and immediately view the statistical distribution of its occurrences across positive versus negative sentences, providing a robust, replicable methodology for investigating evaluative language and authorial stance.
3.2 Mapping Macro-Discourse with Topic Modeling
While concordancers are excellent at uncovering micro-level linguistic patterns, they often fail to capture the macro-level thematic structure of a corpus. CORTEX integrates BERTopic, a state-of-the-art transformer-based topic modeling algorithm. Unlike older keyword frequency models, BERTopic clusters texts based on dense semantic embeddings, grouping discourse that shares conceptual meaning.
In CORTEX, these AI-generated semantic topics are written back into the corpus database as structural metadata. This creates a profound methodological loop: a discourse analyst can use AI to discover the latent thematic structures within a massive corpus, and then immediately return to traditional corpus tools, using those newly discovered semantic topics to filter concordances and collocation networks. This allows researchers to observe how specific lexicogrammatical choices shift depending on the overarching semantic topic being discussed.
3.3 Applied Linguistics and Dynamic Word Profiling
For language educators and material developers, assessing the lexical complexity of a text is a primary concern. CORTEX features a highly dynamic Word Profiler module. While it supports standardized frameworks like the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) and the General Service List (GSL), it allows researchers to easily upload custom, bespoke wordlists. The system calculates coverage statistics and dynamically generates visual badges within the text view. This enables applied linguists to rapidly determine whether a specific text is pedagogically appropriate for a target learner group based on precise vocabulary coverage metrics.
4. A Methodological Case Study: Investigating Evaluative Discourse
To illustrate the linguistic affordances of CORTEX, consider a study within Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) examining how different media outlets frame the concept of 'migration'.
The researcher ingests a corpus of news articles into CORTEX. The ingestion engine automatically parses the XML, retaining metadata such as 'Publisher' and 'Date', while simultaneously tagging the text for POS and lemmatizing the tokens. The researcher's first goal is to map the thematic landscape. By running the BERTopic module, CORTEX clusters the corpus into semantic topics, identifying distinct discourses such as 'Economic Impact', 'Border Security', and 'Humanitarian Aid'.
The researcher then activates the Sentiment Analysis module, assigning an emotional valence to every sentence. Moving to the Concordance tool, the researcher queries the lemma 'migrant'. Instead of manually coding thousands of lines, the researcher uses CORTEX's dynamic filters to isolate instances of 'migrant' occurring specifically within the 'Border Security' topic and filtering exclusively for 'Negative' sentiment sentences. In seconds, the researcher generates a highly specific, evaluatively charged sub-corpus.
Switching to the Collocation module, the researcher analyzes this specific subset, discovering that verbs of movement (e.g., 'flood', 'pour') exhibit exceptionally high Log-Likelihood scores when 'migrant' is framed negatively within security discourse. This entire methodological workflow—moving from raw text to macro-thematic mapping, to automated sentiment filtering, and finally to micro-level phraseological extraction—is conducted seamlessly within a single interface. CORTEX allows the discourse analyst to provide robust, quantitative evidence for complex pragmatic phenomena.
5. Conclusion: A New Paradigm for Empirical Linguistics
CORTEX represents a critical step forward in the evolution of corpus linguistics. For decades, the discipline has excelled at the syntactic and lexical levels of analysis, while struggling to systematically quantify semantics, pragmatics, and macro-discourse. By natively embedding modern Natural Language Processing and AI directly into the traditional corpus workstation, CORTEX proves that this limitation is no longer insurmountable.
The platform does not abandon the foundational methods of the field; rather, it supercharges them. By providing linguists with the ability to filter collocations by AI-generated semantic topics, to measure semantic prosody through automated sentiment tagging, and to ensure lexical generalizability through rigorous dispersion metrics like Juilland’s D, CORTEX significantly expands the empirical horizon of language research.
As linguistic inquiry increasingly demands multi-dimensional analyses that account for both structure and meaning, tools that can synthesize lexicogrammar with computational semantics will become indispensable. CORTEX offers applied linguists, discourse analysts, and lexicographers an open, powerful, and linguistically intuitive platform to investigate language in all its complexity, setting a new standard for methodological rigor in the digital age.
